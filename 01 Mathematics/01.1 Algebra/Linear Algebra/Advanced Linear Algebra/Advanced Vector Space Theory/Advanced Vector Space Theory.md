> [!abstract] Definition
> **Advanced Vector Space Theory** studies additional algebraic structures and constructions built from vector spaces, subspaces, and linear maps. It extends elementary vector-space theory to include duality, quotient constructions, direct-sum decompositions, tensor products, and scalar-valued multilinear forms.

> [!info] Scope
> This subject focuses on the abstract algebraic structure of vector spaces and the relationships between them.
>
> It does not attempt to cover every downstream application of these constructions. Applications to differential geometry, quantum mechanics, optimization, and mechanics belong in their respective subject notes.

> 1. Dual Vector Spaces

> [!important] Conceptual Organization
> - **Duality:** Dual Vector Spaces associate linear functionals with a vector space.
> - **Quotient construction:** Quotient Vector Spaces identify vectors that differ by an element of a selected subspace.
> - **Decomposition:** Direct Sums represent a vector space as a combination of complementary subspaces.
> - **Multilinear construction:** Tensor Products construct vector spaces that encode multilinear relationships.
> - **Scalar-valued structure:** Bilinear Forms and Quadratic Forms introduce additional algebraic structure through scalar-valued functions.

> [!important] Scope Boundary
> These topics are related but not interchangeable:
> - A dual space is constructed from linear functionals.
> - A quotient space is constructed from an equivalence relation induced by a subspace.
> - A direct sum is a particular way to combine or decompose vector spaces.
> - A tensor product is characterized by a universal property for bilinear maps.
> - A bilinear form is linear in each of two arguments.
> - A quadratic form is homogeneous of degree two and is associated with a symmetric bilinear form under suitable field conditions.

> [!success] Foundation Knowledge
> - **Vector Spaces:** Provides the underlying algebraic structure.
> - **Subspaces:** Required for quotient spaces and internal direct sums.
> - **Linear Combinations and Linear Independence:** Support the construction and analysis of bases.
> - **Basis and Dimension:** Enable coordinate representations and finite-dimensional dimension arguments.
> - **Linear Transformations:** Essential for understanding dual maps and induced maps on quotient spaces.
> - **Matrices:** Useful for representing bilinear forms and quadratic forms in finite-dimensional spaces.

> [!success] Downstream Knowledge
> - **Relevant — Annihilators and Duality:** Develops the relationship between subspaces and their corresponding subspaces in the dual space.
> - **Relevant — Multilinear Algebra:** Extends tensor products and multilinear maps to higher-order structures.
> - **Relevant — Symmetric and Alternating Tensors:** Studies tensor symmetries and their algebraic consequences.
> - **Relevant — Matrix Congruence:** Describes changes of coordinates for bilinear and quadratic forms.
> - **Advanced — Exterior Algebra:** Develops alternating tensor constructions and wedge products.
> - **Advanced — Tensor Algebra:** Builds an algebraic structure from tensor products of a vector space.
> - **Advanced — Representation Theory:** Uses vector spaces, dual representations, and tensor products to study algebraic structures.

> [!warning] Mathematical Conditions
> Some relationships depend on the underlying field:
> - The canonical map from a finite-dimensional vector space to its double dual is an isomorphism; in infinite dimensions it is generally injective but not surjective.
> - A quadratic form determines a unique symmetric bilinear form when the field has characteristic different from two.
> - Finite-dimensional dimension formulas should not be applied without checking their hypotheses.
