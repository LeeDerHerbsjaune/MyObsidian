> [!abstract] Definition
> A **Vector Field** is a function that assigns a vector to every point in a specified domain.
>
> In an $n$-dimensional domain, a vector field can be represented as:
>
> $$\mathbf{F}:\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^m$$
>
> In classical three-dimensional Vector Calculus, the most common case is:
>
> $$\mathbf{F}:\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^3\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^3$$

## 1. Mathematical Representation

> [!definition] Three-Dimensional Vector Field
> A vector field in Cartesian coordinates is written as:
>
> $$\mathbf{F}(x,y,z)=
> P(x,y,z)\mathbf{i}
> +Q(x,y,z)\mathbf{j}
> +R(x,y,z)\mathbf{k}$$
>
> Equivalently:
>
> $$\mathbf{F}(x,y,z)=
> \begin{bmatrix}
> P(x,y,z)\\
> Q(x,y,z)\\
> R(x,y,z)
> \end{bmatrix}$$
>
> The component functions $P$, $Q$, and $R$ determine the vector assigned to each spatial point.

> [!example] Example
> Consider:
>
> $$\mathbf{F}(x,y,z)=
> \begin{bmatrix}
> -y\\
> x\\
> 0
> \end{bmatrix}$$
>
> This field assigns vectors tangent to circles centered around the $z$-axis.

## 2. Geometric Interpretation

> [!important] Vector Field Visualization
> A vector field is commonly represented by arrows distributed throughout its domain.
>
> Each arrow indicates:
> - Direction: the orientation of the vector.
> - Magnitude: the length or strength of the vector.
> - Position: the point at which the vector is defined.

> [!note] Field Lines
> A field line is a curve whose tangent direction agrees with the vector field at every point.
>
> If $\mathbf{r}(t)$ is a field line, then:
>
> $$\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}
> =\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t))$$
>
> Field lines provide a geometric representation of the directional structure of a vector field.

## 3. Properties

> [!important] Continuity and Differentiability
> A vector field is continuous if all its component functions are continuous.
>
> It is differentiable if its component functions admit a derivative as a vector-valued mapping.
>
> Its derivative is represented by the Jacobian matrix:
>
> $$D\mathbf{F}=
> \begin{bmatrix}
> \frac{\partial P}{\partial x}&
> \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}&
> \frac{\partial P}{\partial z}\\
> \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}&
> \frac{\partial Q}{\partial y}&
> \frac{\partial Q}{\partial z}\\
> \frac{\partial R}{\partial x}&
> \frac{\partial R}{\partial y}&
> \frac{\partial R}{\partial z}
> \end{bmatrix}$$

> [!important] Magnitude
> The magnitude of a vector field is a scalar field:
>
> $$\|\mathbf{F}\|=
> \sqrt{P^2+Q^2+R^2}$$

## 4. Differential Structure

> [!abstract] Differential Operators
> Three fundamental differential operators characterize important properties of vector fields:
>
> - **Divergence:** Measures local net outward flux density.
> - **Curl:** Measures local rotational tendency.
> - **Gradient:** Generates vector fields from scalar potentials.
>
> The divergence and curl are particularly important for analyzing vector fields.

## 5. Integral Structure

> [!abstract] Integration of Vector Fields
> Vector fields can be integrated over geometric domains:
>
> - **Line Integrals:** Measure circulation or work along curves.
> - **Surface Integrals:** Measure flux through surfaces.
> - **Volume Integrals:** Integrate scalar quantities derived from fields over volumes.

## 6. Knowledge Relationships

### Foundation Knowledge
- **Vector Spaces:** Establishes the mathematical structure of vectors.
- **Functions of Several Variables:** Provides the theory of mappings over multidimensional domains.
- **Euclidean Space:** Provides the geometric domain for classical vector fields.

### Downstream Knowledge
- **Gradient, Divergence, and Curl:** Differential operators acting on fields.
- **Line Integrals:** Measures accumulated along curves.
- **Surface Integrals:** Measures accumulated over surfaces.
- **Conservative Vector Fields:** Studies vector fields generated by scalar potentials.
- **Differential Equations:** Uses vector fields to define spatial and dynamical systems.
