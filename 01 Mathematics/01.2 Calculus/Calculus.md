> [!abstract] Scope
> Calculus is a major branch of mathematics concerned with limits, continuous change, accumulation, and the mathematical analysis of functions.
>
> It provides systematic methods for studying instantaneous rates of change, accumulated quantities, infinite processes, and continuous mathematical models.
>
> Its two principal operations are differentiation and integration, which are connected by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

## 8. Knowledge Relationships

### Foundation Knowledge

- **Real Numbers:** Establishes the number system underlying classical calculus.
- **Set Theory:** Provides the mathematical language for defining sets, functions, and mathematical structures.
- **Mathematical Logic:** Provides the formal framework for mathematical definitions and proofs.
- **Functions:** Provides the fundamental objects studied throughout calculus.
- **Sequences and Series:** Establishes the concepts of convergence and infinite processes.

### Downstream Knowledge

- **Ordinary Differential Equations:** Uses derivatives to describe systems governed by continuous change.
- **Partial Differential Equations:** Extends differential equations to functions of multiple independent variables.
- **Optimization Theory:** Uses derivatives and higher-order information to analyze extrema.
- **Numerical Analysis:** Develops computational methods for approximating derivatives, integrals, and solutions.
- **Probability Theory:** Uses integration and related analytical concepts to study continuous probability distributions.
- **Mathematical Physics:** Applies calculus to formulate and analyze continuous physical systems.

## 9. Conceptual Structure

> [!important] Central Relationship
> Limits provide the foundation for defining derivatives and integrals.
>
> Differentiation and integration are the two principal operations of calculus, connected through the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
>
> Multivariable Calculus generalizes these operations to functions of several variables, while Vector Calculus develops their field-theoretic and geometric formulations.
