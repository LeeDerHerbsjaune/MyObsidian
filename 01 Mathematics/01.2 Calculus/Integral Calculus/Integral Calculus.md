> [!abstract] Definition
> **Integral Calculus** is the branch of calculus concerned with integration: the mathematical process of accumulating quantities, measuring continuous totals, and determining antiderivatives.
>
> It provides a framework for evaluating accumulated quantities, signed areas, volumes, and other continuous aggregates.

## 1. Core Concepts

> [!important] Central Idea
> Integration has two closely connected interpretations:
>
> 1. **Antidifferentiation:** Finding a function whose derivative equals a given function.
> 2. **Accumulation:** Computing the total contribution of a continuously varying quantity over an interval.
>
> These interpretations are connected by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

### 1.1 Indefinite Integration

> [!definition] Indefinite Integral
> An indefinite integral represents the family of all antiderivatives of a function.
>
> It is expressed as:
>
> $$\int f(x)\,dx = F(x)+C$$
>
> where:
> - $f(x)$ is the integrand.
> - $F(x)$ is an antiderivative of $f(x)$.
> - $C$ is an arbitrary constant of integration.

### 1.2 Definite Integration

> [!definition] Definite Integral
> A definite integral represents the accumulated signed contribution of a function over a specified interval.
>
> $$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$$
>
> It is fundamentally defined through limits of sums, with the Riemann integral providing the classical construction.

### 1.3 Fundamental Connection

> [!important] Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
> The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus establishes the relationship between differentiation and definite integration.
>
> If $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and $F'=f$, then:
>
> $$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a)$$
>
> This connection makes it possible to evaluate many definite integrals using antiderivatives.

## 2. Subject Structure

### 2.1 Antiderivatives and Indefinite Integration

> [!note] Antidifferentiation
> These subjects establish the relationship between a function and its antiderivatives.
>
> - **Antiderivatives:** Functions whose derivatives equal a specified function.
> - **Indefinite Integrals:** Families of antiderivatives represented using integral notation.

### 2.2 Definite Integration and Its Foundations

> [!note] Definite Integration
> These subjects establish the mathematical meaning and theoretical foundations of definite integrals.
>
> - **Definite Integrals:** Accumulated signed quantities over intervals.
> - **Riemann Integrals:** A rigorous construction of integration through limits of Riemann sums.

### 2.3 Integration Methods

> [!note] Integration Techniques
> Methods for transforming integrals into forms that can be evaluated more conveniently.
>
> - **Integration Techniques:** Substitution, integration by parts, trigonometric methods, partial fractions, and other techniques.

### 2.4 Extensions of Integration

> [!note] Improper Integration
> Extends definite integration to unbounded intervals and integrands with certain unbounded discontinuities through limiting processes.
>
> - **Improper Integrals:** Convergence and evaluation of integrals defined using limits.

### 2.5 Applications

> [!note] Applications of Integration
> Uses definite integration to calculate accumulated quantities and continuous geometric or physical quantities.
>
> - **Applications of Integration:** Areas, volumes, arc lengths, surface areas, work, mass, and other accumulated quantities.

### 2.6 Unifying Theory

> [!note] Fundamental Theorem
> - **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:** Connects differentiation, antiderivatives, and definite integration.

## 3. Knowledge Relationships

### Foundation Knowledge

> [!info] Essential
> - **Limits:** Required for understanding Riemann sums and the limiting definition of definite integrals.
> - **Functions:** Provide the mathematical objects being integrated.
> - **Differentiation:** Required for understanding antiderivatives and the relationship between differentiation and integration.

> [!info] Relevant
> - **Real Numbers:** Provide the number system underlying intervals, limits, and integration.
> - **Continuity:** Important for classical integrability conditions and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
> - **Sequences and Series:** Useful for understanding approximation and convergence.

### Downstream Knowledge

> [!info] Relevant
> - **Multivariable Calculus:** Extends integration to functions of multiple variables and multiple-dimensional domains.
> - **Vector Calculus:** Develops integration over curves, surfaces, and volumes.
> - **Differential Equations:** Uses integration and antiderivatives in solving differential equations.
> - **Probability Theory:** Uses integration to define probabilities and expectations for continuous random variables.

> [!info] Advanced
> - **Measure-Theoretic Integration:** Generalizes integration beyond the classical Riemann framework.
> - **Lebesgue Integration:** Provides a more general integration theory based on measure and measurable functions.
> - **Functional Analysis:** Studies spaces of integrable functions and operators acting on them.

## 4. Conceptual Relationships

> [!important] Structural Interpretation
> Integral Calculus contains two principal conceptual paths:
>
> **Antidifferentiation**
> - Antiderivatives
> - Indefinite Integrals
>
> **Definite Integration**
> - Riemann Integrals
> - Definite Integrals
> - Improper Integrals
>
> **Connection**
> - Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
>
> **Supporting Methods and Developments**
> - Integration Techniques
> - Applications of Integration
>
> These are conceptual groupings rather than a strict prerequisite sequence. Integration techniques can be used for both indefinite and definite integrals, while the Riemann integral provides a theoretical foundation for classical definite integration.

## 5. Scope and Boundaries

> [!important] Subject Boundary
> Integral Calculus focuses on:
>
> - Antiderivatives and indefinite integrals.
> - Definite integration and its mathematical foundations.
> - Integration methods and convergence of improper integrals.
> - Applications of integration.
> - The relationship between differentiation and integration.
>
> More advanced extensions, such as multiple integrals, line integrals, surface integrals, and measure-theoretic integration, are developed in their respective subjects rather than treated in full here.
