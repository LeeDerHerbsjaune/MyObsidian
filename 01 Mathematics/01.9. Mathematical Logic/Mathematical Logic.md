> [!abstract] Overview
> **Mathematical Logic** is a branch of mathematics that studies formal languages, logical systems, mathematical reasoning, proof structures, and the foundations of mathematical knowledge.
>
> It provides rigorous methods for expressing propositions, defining mathematical structures, deriving conclusions, and investigating the limitations of formal reasoning.
>
> Mathematical logic connects mathematics with computer science through formal verification, automated reasoning, programming language theory, and artificial intelligence.


## 2. Fundamental Concepts

> [!abstract] Basic Logical Concepts
> - [[Proposition]] — a declarative statement that has a truth value.
> - [[Predicate]] — a logical expression whose truth depends on its arguments.
> - [[Truth Value]] — a value representing truth or falsity.
> - [[Quantifier]] — an operator expressing universal or existential conditions.
> - [[Logical Argument]] — a collection of premises and a conclusion.
> - [[Validity]] — a property of arguments whose conclusions follow from their premises.
> - [[Satisfiability]] — the existence of an interpretation under which a formula is true.
> - [[Contradiction]] — a proposition that is false under every interpretation.
> - [[Tautology]] — a proposition that is true under every interpretation.
> - [[Logical Consistency]] — the absence of contradiction in a logical theory.

## 4. Applications

> [!example] Computer Science and Engineering
> Mathematical logic provides theoretical and practical foundations for:
>
> - **Formal Verification:** proving that software and hardware satisfy specified properties.
> - **Automated Reasoning:** deriving conclusions using computational procedures.
> - **Programming Languages:** defining type systems, semantics, and program correctness.
> - **Artificial Intelligence:** knowledge representation and logical inference.
> - **Robotics:** specifying system properties, temporal constraints, and correctness conditions.
> - **Distributed Systems:** reasoning about concurrent processes and system behavior.
> - **Swarm Robotics:** specifying collective behaviors, safety properties, and temporal requirements.
