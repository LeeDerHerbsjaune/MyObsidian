> [!abstract] Overview
> **Set Theory** is a branch of mathematics that studies sets, their elements, relationships, operations, and mathematical properties.
>
> A set is a well-defined collection of distinct objects, called elements or members.
>
> Set theory provides a general mathematical language for describing collections, constructing mathematical structures, and formalizing many areas of mathematics.
>
> It is also one of the principal foundational frameworks used in modern mathematics.

> [!abstract] Core Notation
> Let \(A\) and \(B\) be sets.
>
> - Membership: \(x \in A\)
> - Non-membership: \(x \notin A\)
> - Subset: \(A \subseteq B\)
> - Proper subset: \(A \subsetneq B\)
> - Union: \(A \cup B\)
> - Intersection: \(A \cap B\)
> - Difference: \(A \setminus B\)
> - Power set: \(\mathcal{P}(A)\)
> - Cartesian product: \(A \times B\)
> - Cardinality: \(|A|\)

> [!example] Mathematics and Engineering
> Set theory is used throughout mathematics and technical disciplines.
>
> - **Mathematical Foundations:** constructing numbers, relations, functions, and mathematical structures.
> - **Discrete Mathematics:** describing collections, relations, graphs, and combinatorial objects.
> - **Computer Science:** defining data structures, types, databases, and formal specifications.
> - **Probability Theory:** defining sample spaces, events, and measurable collections.
> - **Topology:** defining topological spaces through collections of open sets.
> - **Robotics:** representing configuration spaces, workspace regions, obstacle sets, and robot populations.
> - **Swarm Robotics:** describing robot populations, agent subsets, communication neighborhoods, and collective states.
